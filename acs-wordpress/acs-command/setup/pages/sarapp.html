Search and rescue first

Command information that reaches the field—and comes back.

Arc-Horizon is being designed around the full search operation: building teams, assigning work, maintaining an operational map, following progress, receiving field reports, tracking critical information, and preserving the incident record.

The same foundation can support other incidents and planned events without turning every operation into the same workflow.

The idea behind Arc-Horizon

Plan. Coordinate.
Respond. Together.

Arc-Horizon is not meant to create another isolated command-post system. It is meant to connect the people developing the plan, coordinating the operation, and carrying out the work in the field.

01

Plan

Turn objectives, intelligence, geography, risks, resources, and operational priorities into work that can be understood and assigned.

02

Coordinate

Keep command staff, partner agencies, support functions, and field teams working from the same operational picture.

03

Respond

Move assignments and critical information into the field, then return status, observations, and results to command.

04

Together

The value comes from closing the gap between those four groups—not from giving each of them another separate tool.

01

Command

Incident organization, personnel, teams, resources, operational status, alerts, and a common command picture.

02

Assignments

Task development, briefing, team assignment, status tracking, field progress, and operational closeout.

03

GIS

Search planning, operational layers, assignments, clues, hazards, drawing tools, and map exchange.

04

Information

Field reports, subjects, clues, leads, weather, safety information, agency coordination, and situation awareness.

05

Planning

Objectives, strategies, tactics, meetings, resource needs, operational periods, and action-plan development.

06

Documentation

ICS forms, logs, assignment records, situation reports, and an incident record built from work already being tracked.

Command application

A complete working environment for the command post.

The Windows desktop application is being designed for incident command, section staff, planners, dispatchers, and other personnel who need the complete operational picture and the tools to manage it.

  • Incident and organizational management
  • Team, task, and resource boards
  • Operational GIS and search planning
  • Planning-cycle and documentation support

Companion field app

The team’s connection to the incident.

The companion app gives field personnel a focused mobile experience without reproducing the entire command application on a smaller screen.

  • Team check-in and assignment details
  • Maps and task information
  • Status and narrative reporting
  • Hazards, messages, and field intelligence
